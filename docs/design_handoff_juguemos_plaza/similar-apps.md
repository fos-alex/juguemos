# Juguemos — Similar Apps

**Version:** 0.1 · September 2026 · Owner: Alex Otero

*Research on how similar apps look and feel, done for the basic branding in 0.1 (JUG-10). It covers design and styling.*

---

## 1. Summary

The question behind this research: should Juguemos look like a kids' app, since it's about playing with little kids, or like a tool for the adults who hold the phone?

**Answer: design for the adults, and let the playfulness come from the words, colour and handwork.** This matches the design brief: "a tool for grown-ups, about play".

- **Every parent-facing play app styles itself for adults.** Childlike touches are small accents, never the whole look.
- **Story apps look like kids' apps only when the child watches the screen.** When a parent reads, the screen gets out of the way.
- **Kid styling tells a child the phone is for them.** Children are drawn to cartoons and animation, and adults tend to dislike them. That works against *Screens off, play on*.
- **Buenos Aires children's culture is already crafted and artful.** Its best picture books, music and TV speak to adults too. Playful for local kids doesn't mean cartoonish.
- **There's open ground.** No Spanish-first Latin American app is warm, grown-up and crafted. The regional player, Kinedu, is clinical, and the Latin American AI story apps copy the English template.

Also looked at: TinyPlay (photos of real kids and household objects, and a "tired-parent mode") and Roo, a UK app (a kangaroo mascot).

**Two camps.**

- **Clinical expert trackers:** Kinedu, BabySparks, Huckleberry. Blue or green on white, geometric sans, progress rings, milestone copy.
- **Warm lifestyle:** Lovevery (branded by Pentagram), Tinkergarten, Willo. Earthy colour, candid photos or editorial illustration, custom or serif type.

The newest apps, like Willo and TinyPlay, lean warm, with cream backgrounds, soft serifs and calm copy.

**Most copy sells certainty and fear of falling behind:** "Know.", "the best start", "invest now". Vroom's brand guidelines are the clear exception, and the closest to the Juguemos constitution:

- Its materials may not be used on anything meant for children.
- It never corrects parents and never talks about bad outcomes.
- Photos show a caregiver and child playing with everyday things, and no posed stock photos.

**Kidoc is the closest match in function.** It gives one idea at a time and remembers past activities. It isn't local, isn't in Spanish, and doesn't know the family's toys by name.

**Avoid "sad beige".** The all-neutral minimalist look for babies is now widely mocked, and younger parents want real colour. The safe middle is a warm adult tool with earthy, confident colour.

**Kid styling follows the child's eyes.** Where the child watches (Oscar, Storytime AI), apps are bright, illustrated and use mascots. Where an adult runs the screen, it steps back: Readmio removes art from the reading view, and Moshi hides graphics during playback. Readmio keeps its childlike touches (the mascot and cover art) to the screens where a story is chosen.

**There's a gap in Spanish.** No Latin American app is built around a parent reading a text-only story aloud. The Latin American AI apps copy the English-language template.

## 4. Brands that are playful for grown-ups

| Brand | What to learn from it |
|---|---|
| **Pok Pok** (a kids' app that also speaks to parents) | Founding rule: parents shouldn't need to mute it in a restaurant, so no jingles. Hand-drawn art where "everything could jitter a little bit". Only 11 colours plus white in the whole app |
| **Headspace** (2024 rebrand) | The smiley now shows a range of moods, not just joy. A custom typeface that can "flex from playful to clinical". Warm orange chosen against a "dreary sea of blues and greys". Its illustrator aims for "friendly and witty… avoiding being too childish or cartoony" |
| **Mailchimp** (Collins, 2018) | Hand-drawn illustration borrowing from outsider art, paired with Cooper Light, a 1920s serif. "A precise balance between the sophisticated and the surreal" |
| **Oatly** | Hand-drawn packaging so it doesn't feel corporate. Most of the playfulness lives in dry, funny copy |
| **Tiimo** | Animations "move slowly and subtly, designed to guide attention rather than demand it". Gentle nudges, no shame |
| **Duolingo** | Its rounded, bright style started in its kids' app and moved to the main app. Chubby type, saturated green and 3D-looking buttons. It works for a game, but it's the look to avoid for a calm tool |
| **Finch** | Openly cute, and it works with adults through warmth and a total absence of shame. No source on whether adults find it childish |

**Design trends, 2025–26.**

- **"Imperfect by design"** (grain, hand-drawn marks, naive drawing) is a reaction to smooth AI-made visuals. What gives it value is a real, named maker. Imitation wobble doesn't.
- **Warm neutrals** (clay, sand, sage) are replacing cool greys for trust and wellbeing products.
- **"Dopamine design"** (saturated maximalism) suits youthful brands better than ones that depend on trust.
- **Neobrutalism** can overwhelm users and hurt accessibility (NN/g).

**What reads childish:** heavy animation and sound, garish saturation, glossy 3D, cartoon mascots front and centre, chubby rounded display type.

**What reads playful but grown-up:** a limited palette, visible handwork, a serious serif or neutral sans with one expressive detail, slow motion, and wit in the copy.

## 5. Buenos Aires children's culture

The local scene is crafted and artful, and made to be enjoyed by adults as well as children. It's a better reference than any app.

- **Isol.** Black line and flat colour, usually no more than three colours, with influences from Dubuffet, Schiele, Miró and comics. Her books have "several levels of reading" for children and adults. She won the ALMA prize and was an Andersen finalist.
- **Calibroscopio.** A 2026 finalist for Bologna's prize for best children's publisher. It wants to bring back the time when "illustration was artisanal and each brushstroke conveyed a unique emotion".
- **Limonero.** Named best publisher for South and Central America at Bologna in 2019. It rejects "children's books that make adults yawn".
- **Ediciones del Eclipse** (now closed). Published Argentina's first picture-book collection, including Isol.
- **Pequeño Editor.** Board books and die-cut books by named illustrators.
- **Pakapaka**, the public children's TV channel.
  - It aimed for "a richer colour palette than the usual flat tones", slower pacing, and work only by Argentine illustrators and animators.
  - Its 2020 identity, *Inventar el mundo*, won the national Sello de Buen Diseño.
  - It was rebranded again in 2024–25, and the current identity isn't verified.
- **Canticuénticos.** Built on Latin American rhythms. Their picture books are illustrated in collage by Estrellita Caracol (Natalia Spadaro), out of a stated love of handwork.

## 6. What parents respond to

- **Design shapes trust.** In Stanford's web credibility study (2,684 people), 46.1% judged credibility partly on visual design. Attractive interfaces are also *perceived* as easier to use (the aesthetic-usability effect).
- **For parents specifically,** an app's design shaped first impressions of its quality, and so whether they downloaded it (Bhandari et al., 2014).
- **Style signals who an app is for.** In Nielsen Norman Group testing, a six-year-old dismissed a site as "for babies… You can tell because of the cartoons and trains". Children like animation and sound, while adults usually dislike them.
- **Kid styling may draw the child to the phone.** No study tests this directly. The indirect evidence is suggestive:
  - Children engage with animation and sound.
  - Preschoolers trust and pay attention to familiar cartoon characters.
  - Toddlers differ in how easily attention-grabbing digital content pulls them in.
- **Not found:** any survey on whether parents feel embarrassed using a babyish app in public. Pok Pok's restaurant rule is the nearest signal.

## 7. Designing the night reading screen

- **Light at night.** Any light suppresses melatonin, blue light the most and dim red light the least. Reading on a light-emitting e-reader delayed melatonin timing by about 1.5 hours compared with a printed book. The American Academy of Ophthalmology recommends night mode.
- **Colour.** Keep brightness low with amber and brown tones, and avoid bright white and blue.
- **Surfaces.** Material Design recommends dark grey (#121212) rather than pure black, desaturated colours, and at least 4.5:1 contrast. Tinting the greys warm is our inference, not a source's.
- **Text.** Light mode is more legible for most people, and the gap grows as text gets smaller (NN/g). Dark mode therefore needs large text. Design blogs also suggest one weight heavier on dark backgrounds.
- **No competitor has a warm night theme.** Readmio reportedly offers only a black-and-white inversion. A warm night mode would set Juguemos apart.

## 8. What this means for Juguemos

**Five levers for playful without childish:**

1. **A small warm palette with one confident accent.** Neither rainbow saturation nor sad beige.
2. **Visible handwork:** chalk lines, cardboard, collage, grain, a slight wobble. No glossy 3D and no mascot. Illustration itself belongs to JUG-17 in 0.2.
3. **Grown-up type with one playful detail.** Chubby rounded display type is the lever that reads most childish.
4. **Quiet motion and no sound,** following Pok Pok's restaurant rule. This also avoids pulling the toddler to the screen.
5. **The wit lives in the copy.** The Rioplatense voice in the design brief already does this, and the idea itself is the delight.

**The brand directions in JUG-10.**

- **Tiza** (chalk and cardboard) and **Cuento** (a children's book for the parents) fit the research best.
- In **Plaza**, test the rounded friendly type most carefully.

**References to borrow from:**

- **Vroom's brand rules,** for tone and imagery.
- **Readmio,** for keeping art off the reading screen.
- **Pok Pok,** for restraint.
- **Headspace,** for a warm expert without the clinical look.
- **Isol and Limonero,** for local, crafted playfulness.

**References to avoid:**

- **Kinedu and BabySparks,** for clinical milestone pressure.
- **Oscar Stories and the Latin American AI story apps,** for the child-facing, AI-illustrated template.
- **Duolingo,** for gamified cartoon energy.
